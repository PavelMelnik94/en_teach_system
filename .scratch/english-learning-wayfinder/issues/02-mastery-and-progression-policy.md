## Question

What should mastery mean in this workspace, what evidence threshold permits moving on, and what bounded revisit/escalation rule prevents both curriculum-driven gaps and infinite polishing?

## Answer

Mastery is an evidence profile, not a single linear status for a whole capability. Track evidence separately for each relevant `context × mode × support` slice. The workspace may use `introduced`, `supported`, `independent`, `transferable`, and `retained` as useful evidence states, but they are not a mandatory ladder and need not advance together. For example, reading may be independent while listening is supported and speaking is introduced.

Progression uses a local prerequisite floor rather than perfection. A small amount of adjacent material may be introduced when the prerequisite is understood well enough to avoid conceptual misunderstanding and can be applied independently in a familiar context. Delayed retrieval is not a universal gate for adding any new material, because waiting for a meaningful delay would unnecessarily stall learning. However, the prerequisite is not considered stable or closed until delayed evidence exists; critical prerequisites may require a stricter floor before expansion.

Every evidence event is recorded at task level as:

`task outcome + mode + support + context novelty + error type + delayed/transfer evidence`.

Accuracy can be retained as a local signal where useful, but it cannot independently determine mastery or progression. Recognition, for example, does not stand in for independent speaking or listening.

The progression loop is bounded and local:

1. Add a small adjacent challenge once the relevant prerequisite floor is met.
2. Keep unresolved slices in delayed and interleaved review; do not mark them mastered prematurely.
3. When a recurring prerequisite failure appears, run targeted remediation for that specific gap, then retest with a changed prompt.
4. If the floor is restored, continue forward; rollback means local remediation, never replaying an arbitrary earlier block or "chapters".

No universal attempt count, exercise count, or rigid SRS interval is part of the policy. Research supports delayed effortful retrieval and adaptive spacing, but not a universal threshold or schedule for this learner. The anti-polishing boundary is therefore the prerequisite floor plus continued review, not perfection.
