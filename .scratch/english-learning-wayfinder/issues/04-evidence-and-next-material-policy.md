## Question

How should `/teach` choose the next material from observed evidence: error taxonomy, retrieval/use performance, uncertainty, mission relevance, and readiness for a bounded learning horizon?

## Answer

`/teach` chooses a bounded learning objective, not the next chapter or a prewritten topic sequence. The objective has this shape:

`capability outcome + context + target mode/support + prerequisite assumptions + evidence to collect + planned follow-up`.

Grammar or vocabulary may be the direct objective when an identified prerequisite gap blocks communicative progression; the policy does not force every objective to be a larger scenario.

Evidence is interpreted qualitatively in four tiers:

- **Blocker** — prerequisite failure prevents the nearest communicative outcome or makes the adjacent material unusable.
- **Important weakness** — materially limits the outcome but does not block a viable next step.
- **Non-blocking weakness** — a real gap that can remain in review while progress continues.
- **Optional enrichment** — useful improvement without a near-term functional payoff.

A low result alone does not promote a weakness to the top tier. A blocker may temporarily displace other work; otherwise the system avoids always selecting the weakest slice.

Candidate next steps are evaluated through an ordinal decision check, not an artificial weighted formula:

1. Is there a blocker for the nearest capability objective?
2. Is a delayed/retention gap important for the intended use horizon?
3. Is a recognition-only or supported slice ready to be raised toward production or transfer?
4. Does the candidate correct a meaningful listening/speaking/comprehension/production imbalance?
5. Is it inside the learner's current cognitive load and productive zone?
6. Can an already useful function transfer to another context instead of being relearned as a separate topic?
7. Which candidate gives the greatest immediate functional gain, with priority to direct Life/Social/Work/Interview relevance when candidates are otherwise comparable?

This ordering is a set of decision checks, not a rigid rule that always lets one item defeat all later context. The selected step must preserve prerequisites, avoid new foundational debt, and be explainable in one short rationale: `why now → evidence that triggered it → what will improve or be tested`.

Session composition is adaptive. A 10-minute session may spend all its time on one remediation, retrieval, listening, or production target. A longer session may combine a small delayed review, one main remediation/deepening/adjacent objective, and a listening/speaking/transfer/repair probe. No fixed template is required.

Constraints retained from the capability and mastery policies:

- Work and frontend scenarios can enter early at accessible language complexity.
- Listening receives explicit priority when it trails other modes.
- Repair appears opportunistically during breakdowns and sometimes as focused practice.
- Communicative functions transfer across contexts where possible.
- Interleaving follows a minimal representation and targets discrimination/transfer; it is not random mixing.
- Delayed retrieval and varied follow-up are evidence signals, not a universal fixed SRS schedule.
