## Question

Which evidence-backed spacing, retrieval, delayed review, and interleaving principles should govern adaptive practice, and how can they fit 10–40 minute sessions without importing a rigid flashcard curriculum?

## Answer

Review is an adaptive opportunity selected from `/teach`'s existing learning state, not a separate scheduler, calendar, database, or manual backlog. The policy distinguishes five different actions:

- **Immediate practice** — supported recovery during the same learning episode; useful when stuck, but not evidence of retention.
- **Delayed retrieval** — effortful recall after a meaningful interval, with less cueing; the primary retention signal.
- **Interleaved practice** — choosing among related/confusable forms, contexts, or skills rather than repeating one blocked pattern.
- **Transfer check** — using the capability in a changed context, prompt, interlocutor, or mode.
- **Remediation** — short targeted explanation/practice for a classified recurring or conceptual gap, followed by a changed-prompt retest.

The review candidate is chosen qualitatively from these signals:

- foundational prerequisite or blocker status;
- recurring conceptual or production error;
- unstable, provisional, or low-confidence evidence;
- failure after delay, increased support, or missing transfer;
- high-value Life/Social/Work/Interview capability;
- material needed soon by the current bounded objective;
- mode-specific weakness (for example reading stable but fast listening unstable);
- time since use, interpreted relative to the intended use horizon rather than a universal interval.

Stable material gradually loses priority when delayed retrieval succeeds with reduced support, the relevant task outcome is preserved, and varied or transfer evidence no longer shows a recurring failure. It is not deleted or declared globally retained: each mode/context slice can decay or strengthen independently. Stable material returns opportunistically through low-cue retrieval or transfer, while unstable material returns sooner and with only enough support to recover useful performance.

If delayed retrieval fails, `/teach` does not infer that all learning is lost. It first records the failure and classifies it (conceptual, recurring grammar, vocabulary, listening/parsing, production, pronunciation, or task/attention issue). It then gives only the shortest necessary remediation, retries a changed prompt, updates confidence and the slice evidence, and raises near-term review priority when warranted. Repeated explanation-reading without retrieval does not close the gap.

Interleaving is introduced only after each candidate has a minimal usable representation. When a learner is still introducing a form or cannot perform it even with constrained support, use a short blocked/constrained phase. Once related forms have enough representation, interleave deliberately to force discrimination and transfer—especially for forms that regularly compete—without randomly mixing unrelated material or adding irrelevant cognitive load.

Transfer strengthens evidence but does not replace delayed evidence. A changed-context success may show flexibility while retention remains provisional; a delayed success in the original context may show access without transfer. Both are recorded separately.

Review is bounded by the current session and objective, not by clearing a historical queue:

- a 10-minute session may be entirely one high-value retrieval/recovery/transfer review;
- a 20–40 minute session may reserve a small review slice alongside the main bounded objective when that review protects a prerequisite or high-value capability;
- if review would crowd out all functional progress and no blocker is present, defer lower-tier review and continue forward.

No universal day-count sequence, expanding SRS algorithm, item quota, or review percentage is specified. The research supports delayed effortful retrieval, spacing relative to the desired retention horizon, and purposeful rather than random interleaving; it does not support one schedule or mastery threshold for this learner. During a session, the learner sees only a concise reason such as: “We’re returning to X because its last delayed attempt was unstable three sessions ago.”
