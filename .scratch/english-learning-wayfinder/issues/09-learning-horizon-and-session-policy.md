## Question

What is the adaptive planning horizon and session policy: how `/teach` chooses a 10–40 minute session, balances urgent function with foundation, and updates the plan from actual performance rather than calendar or lesson number?

## Answer

Use four horizons without adding a planning artifact:

1. **Long-term mission/capabilities** — stable purpose and success outcomes in `MISSION.md`.
2. **Near-term direction** — a short hypothesis about one or a few related capability areas in `LEARNING-STATE.md`, without dates, lesson lists, or guaranteed sequence. Revisit it when meaningful evidence changes the situation, not after every session.
3. **Current bounded objective** — the ticket-04 objective in `LEARNING-STATE.md`: outcome, context, target mode/support, prerequisites, evidence to collect, follow-up, and brief rationale.
4. **Session purpose** — an ephemeral decision for the current interaction: acquisition, remediation, retrieval, listening, production, transfer, repair, diagnostic probing, or a contextually useful mix. Persist it only when the session changes durable evidence or objective state.

There is no separate persistent `learning cycle`. The current objective has the lifecycle `selected → active → evidence check → continue / remediate / transfer / review / replace`. It is not a numbered cycle and has no fixed lesson count.

## Session start and composition

At resume, `/teach` reads `MISSION.md` and `LEARNING-STATE.md`, checks high-value review/follow-up needs, notes available time and modality, and selects the smallest useful session purpose for the current objective. It does not perform a long planning ritual or re-plan merely for variety. If time is not stated, assume a normal 20–40 minute session. If the learner says there are about 10 minutes, create a complete micro-session without an additional questionnaire; ask about time only when the choice genuinely cannot be made otherwise.

Composition follows evidence and cognitive load, not a fixed template. A micro-session may be one high-value delayed retrieval, remediation, listening, production, transfer, or repair task. A normal session may combine a small review with the main objective and a transfer/listening/speaking probe, but any component may be omitted. Longer voluntary sessions can extend the same objective or add a changed prompt; extra time never opens an unrelated chapter automatically.

If audio/voice is unavailable, text work may continue where useful, but listening/speaking slices remain `unknown / not tested` or retain their last valid evidence; they do not receive negative evidence. An audio-dependent objective is deferred. Repeated deferral caused by the environment is recorded as a capability-coverage gap, distinct from learner weakness, and may change near-term direction when it materially blocks progress.

## Adaptive difficulty, review, and relevance

The session protects the current productive zone. Limit simultaneous novelty, new concepts, mode difficulty, real-time pressure, and prerequisite load qualitatively. If a task is too hard, lower novelty or support demands, decompose the outcome, probe the prerequisite, or remediate the specific gap. If evidence is rapidly strong, fade support, change prompt/context, switch mode, run transfer, schedule delayed retrieval, or stop; do not repeat identical items to fill time.

Review competes with new functional progress through the ticket-04 decision checks. A high-value blocker or prerequisite may make review the whole session. Otherwise select only enough high-priority retrieval/transfer to protect important skills and defer lower-tier backlog so functional progress continues. Do not add new grammar or vocabulary merely because time remains; use transfer, spontaneous production, listening, delayed material, or finish instead.

Interest and urgency are tie-breakers among pedagogically viable candidates: prefer Life in Tbilisi, Social, Work/Frontend, and Interview relevance, while varying prompts beyond memorized personal answers. Work scenarios enter early but remain language-bounded and capability-relevant; they do not create a separate business-English track.

## Stopping, continuity, and recovery

Continue the current objective across sessions unless evidence shows a blocker, prerequisite failure, unexpected strength, mode imbalance, or changed priority. End a session when its bounded purpose is met, enough evidence has been collected, cognitive load is high, time is over, or further practice has diminishing returns. A normal learner-facing close is only:

`what improved → what remains unstable → likely next focus`.

The underlying state update follows ticket 07: meaningful evidence, objective status, blockers/weaknesses, review follow-ups, unknown coverage, and next direction. A missed day, week, or longer pause does not reset a curriculum or streak. Resume with the saved state, a small high-value retrieval/probe, and confidence adjustment as evidence warrants.

After baseline, diagnostic probing and the first objective can coexist. Unknown slices are probed opportunistically when they affect a useful decision; there is no diagnostic curriculum gate.

## Anti-curriculum guard

The policy is invalid if future lessons can be enumerated months ahead. Near-term direction remains a revisable hypothesis, current objectives are replaced only by evidence or a real mission change, and extra session time never authorizes an unrelated next chapter. Visible progress is evidence change—more independent production, delayed retrieval, transfer, listening without transcript, or successful repair—not XP, streaks, or lesson counts.
