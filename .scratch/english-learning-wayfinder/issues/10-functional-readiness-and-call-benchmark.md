## Question

What observable evidence defines readiness for progressively less-supported life/social exchanges, recruiter and technical interviews, and participation in a natural multi-speaker frontend team call, including comprehension, response, repair, and intelligibility?

## Answer

Functional readiness is a capability-specific interpretation of the existing evidence profile, not a second progress model and not a binary global badge. A human-readable view may say `not demonstrated`, `supported`, `independently functional`, `functional under variation`, or `evidence insufficient/stale`; these are derived from mode, support, context novelty, task outcome, transfer, delayed evidence, repair, confidence, and provenance.

Readiness never requires perfection. Effective repair, pauses, clarification requests, non-native pronunciation, partial comprehension, and imperfect grammar count as successful performance when the communicative outcome is recovered. A capability is not ready merely because a related mode is strong: written technical explanation does not prove real-time speaking, and reading technical documentation does not prove listening readiness.

## Capability benchmarks

- **Life:** handle an unprepared everyday request; describe an unknown object or problem; explain an unusual request; understand the actionable part of the response; ask a follow-up; notice misunderstanding; repair it; and reach a usable outcome. The restaurant request is a validation shape, not a memorized curriculum item.
- **Social:** initiate interaction, answer an unexpected question, sustain several turns, ask a meaningful follow-up, tell a short story, give an opinion/reason, understand a reaction, handle an unknown word, and recover from misunderstanding. Monologue length is not the benchmark.
- **Work:** progressively demonstrate direct-question comprehension, status and blocker reports, clarification and confirmation, bug/expected-vs-actual explanation, simple solution proposals, cause/effect reasoning, alternatives/trade-offs, disagreement, follow-up handling, and code-review discussion. These are reusable outcomes across contexts, not a mandatory ladder.
- **Interview:** treat recruiter/HR, experience/project discussion, technical reasoning, architecture/trade-off discussion, and unexpected follow-ups as separate capabilities. Prepared answers support acquisition, but readiness requires changed prompts and novel follow-up response.

## Team-call benchmark

Team-call readiness is measured across manipulable dimensions, not a CEFR jump or one fixed simulation. Increase or decrease number of speakers, turn length/count, speech rate, accent familiarity, overlap/interruption, topic familiarity, vocabulary novelty, technical density, contextual dependency, transcript/support, and response pressure according to evidence.

Listening evidence remains separate for gist, action-relevant detail, parsing/segmentation, known language in connected speech, speaker/turn tracking, recognizing an addressed question, context retention across turns, actionable understanding, and response readiness. Participation evidence includes short response, clarification, confirmation, relevant addition, blocker report, follow-up answer, polite disagreement, simple reasoning, turn entry, and recovery after missed information.

Intermediate simulations can move from controlled roleplay and short one-speaker updates through changed multi-turn exchanges, disagreement recognition, addressed follow-ups, and missed-phrase recovery toward multi-speaker, accent-varied, overlapping, technical team-call-like input. These dimensions are probes, not a required sequence. Complex architecture discussion is a higher capability, not a gate for all English-speaking work.

## Evidence and provenance

Readiness requires converging evidence: varied prompts and contexts, reduced support, mode-specific outcomes, changed/novel transfer, and delayed follow-up when stability materially matters. A single successful simulation or repeated identical script cannot establish it. Simulation evidence is labelled simulated; real-world evidence records its source and confidence. Post-event self-report is useful evidence with its provenance and confidence, but does not automatically outrank directly observed performance.

Repair is valid performance. The benchmark asks whether the learner noticed a breakdown, selected an appropriate repair, restored the outcome, and could reuse the strategy under variation—not whether they avoided asking for repetition. Later failures lower confidence or raise review/remediation priority for the affected slice; they do not erase all progression.

## Practical thresholds

**Start applying for English-language roles:** the learner can complete a basic recruiter/HR exchange with reasonable preparation, explain experience and a project, handle ordinary follow-ups, describe a limitation/blocker, request clarification, and recover communication. Gaps in team calls or advanced architecture can continue in parallel; they are not a prerequisite to applying or interviewing.

**Function in an English-speaking team:** the learner can understand action-relevant instructions and status updates, report status/blockers, ask for clarification and confirm understanding, sustain necessary several-turn interaction with one or more speakers, answer follow-ups, add relevant information, express simple disagreement/reasoning, and recover after missed speech. The benchmark tolerates imperfect language and support that is still reasonable for the work context; it does not claim full multi-speaker architecture readiness.

CEFR may calibrate or communicate broad expectations and descriptors, but `CEFR B1/B2` never implies work or team-call readiness. Readiness gaps feed ticket-04 selection: weak follow-up listening should produce follow-up/listening objectives, not more prepared status monologues; weak repair should produce targeted breakdown practice; strong written evidence with missing audio evidence should leave the audio slice unknown.

## State representation

`LEARNING-STATE.md` stores only readiness-relevant evidence and distance to current practical targets: the affected capability/context, mode-specific evidence, support/novelty, outcome, repair, confidence/provenance, delayed/transfer status, and the next gap to close. It does not duplicate the benchmark framework or create a readiness database.
