## Question

What progression and evidence should connect grammar, vocabulary/chunks, controlled-to-spontaneous speaking, controlled-to-natural listening, pronunciation for intelligibility, and repair strategies from early A1 work toward real conversations and team calls?

## Answer

Modes are coordinated views of the same capability, not four independent courses. For each relevant capability slice, `/teach` keeps separate evidence for comprehension, production, interaction, and transfer while grammar, vocabulary/chunks, pronunciation, and repair explain or enable the outcome. A stronger reading result never automatically upgrades listening or speaking.

Listening and speaking receive priority for this mission, followed by reading, with writing used selectively. The next mode is chosen by the current capability objective and imbalance: familiar language can be used for a new listening demand, or known listening content for a new speaking demand. Reading/grammar-heavy evidence should trigger more audio and interaction, not more text by default.

Listening progression is a manipulable set of dimensions rather than a fixed curriculum: controlled short input → familiar language in natural-ish speech → reductions and connected speech → short less-controlled exchanges → multiple speakers, accent variation, speed, overlap, and topic load → technical/team-call-like excerpts. Change one or a few dimensions at a time and keep the task outcome comprehensible enough to produce evidence. Do not expose an A1 learner directly to unmodified calls merely to demonstrate difficulty.

Listening evidence is recorded separately for:

- gist;
- action-relevant detail;
- word-boundary/segmentation and parsing;
- recognition of known vocabulary/grammar in connected speech;
- response readiness when a question or turn is addressed to the learner.

Valid outcomes are task-dependent: gist sufficient, key detail sufficient, actionable understanding, or full comprehension where the task truly requires it. Transcript/subtitle support is staged: use it to acquire or diagnose a passage, remove it for retrieval and fresh listening, then optionally replay after the transcript to connect print with sound. Transcript success alone is not listening evidence.

Speaking progresses by support and task demands, not by a mandatory ladder: model/imitation, constrained response, sentence production, short communicative outcome, semi-controlled interaction, changed prompt, and spontaneous interaction are available probes. Evidence must include unexpected questions, clarification, correction, negotiation of meaning, brief reasoning, follow-up, turn-taking, and recovery—not only prepared monologues. Repetition can build fluency, but changed prompts/interlocutors test transfer. Support is the help actually required, and it is reduced gradually as independence grows; response-time pressure becomes more realistic without universal second thresholds.

Repair is a first-class cross-cutting capability from the beginning. Use fixed phrases as initial tools, then test flexible choices in unexpected breakdowns. Repair is trained both opportunistically when a real misunderstanding appears and deliberately through controlled communication-breakdown tasks. The intervention matches the failure:

- not heard → repeat/slower/segment request;
- heard but not parsed → replay, boundary/reduction focus, or clarification;
- parsed but meaning unclear → ask for another explanation or example;
- meaning understood but response unavailable → time-gain, paraphrase, or simpler formulation;
- vocabulary gap → description, example, or circumlocution;
- learner not understood → self-correction, reformulation, confirmation check;
- partial understanding → summarize/check the understood part and request the missing detail.

Record whether the learner noticed the breakdown, initiated repair, selected an appropriate strategy, restored the outcome, and transferred the strategy to a new context.

Pronunciation is targeted only when it affects intelligibility/comprehensibility or listening perception. Prioritize high-impact sounds, stress, rhythm, prosody, and chunk boundaries; do not spend review capacity on accent features that do not limit understanding. Speaking and listening work can reinforce each other, but pronunciation is not a native-accent gate.

Work enters early through language-bounded outcomes—name a blocker, report status, ask for clarification, confirm understanding—before complex architecture debate. Life, Social, Work, and Interview contexts reuse common functions such as clarification, explanation, cause/effect, and trade-off; transfer between contexts is preferred to duplicating a topic.

Team-call readiness grows through increasingly demanding evidence: direct question, one-speaker status update, short exchange gist, disagreement recognition, addressed follow-up, missed-phrase recovery, and several-turn context. These are probes, not a mandatory ladder. Later simulations should vary speakers, turns, accent, overlap, and unexpectedness while recording main idea, actionable details, turn entry, repair, blocker explanation, and recovery.

When no audio-capable tool is available, text tasks may support grammar/reading/planning but must not simulate listening or speaking confidence. The evidence state remains `unknown / insufficiently probed` for those modes, with a future audio probe as follow-up.

For each cycle, `/teach` can therefore choose: mode, target support, whether to move recognition toward production, whether to add novelty or real-time interaction, which repair strategy matches the failure, and whether pronunciation is a functional bottleneck. It uses the same bounded-objective and slice-level evidence policies from tickets 01–05; it does not create Grammar, Listening, Speaking, or Pronunciation courses.
