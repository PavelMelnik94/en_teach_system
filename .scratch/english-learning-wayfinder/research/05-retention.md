# Retention, retrieval, spacing, and interleaving

Research note for **Retention review and interleaving policy**. This is evidence
for a later decision; it is not a fixed flashcard schedule.

## Findings

1. **Delayed retrieval is a better learning signal than immediate familiarity.**
   In a controlled foreign-language vocabulary experiment, repeated study after
   an item was recalled did not improve delayed recall, whereas repeated testing
   produced a large delayed-retention benefit. Learners' predictions were also
   poorly aligned with later performance. This supports treating successful
   retrieval after a delay as evidence, rather than lesson completion or
   recognition during explanation. [Karpicke & Roediger, 2008, *Science*, DOI
   10.1126/science.1152408](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1152408).

2. **Spacing should be judged against the intended retention horizon.** A large
   quantitative synthesis of verbal-learning experiments found that the
   retention interval and the gap between learning episodes interact: the gap
   that best supports retention grows as the desired retention interval grows.
   Therefore, one universal interval is not evidence-based. [Cepeda et al.,
   2006, *Psychological Bulletin*, DOI
   10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354).

3. **The L2 evidence supports spacing, especially for delayed tests, but does
   not require an expanding algorithm.** A meta-analysis of 48 second-language
   experiments (3,411 participants) reported a medium-to-large spacing effect;
   shorter spacing was about as effective on immediate tests but less effective
   on delayed tests than longer spacing. Equal and expanding schedules were
   statistically equivalent in that synthesis. Effects varied with target,
   number of sessions, practice/activity type, feedback timing, and retention
   interval. [Kim & Webb, 2022, *Language Learning*, DOI
   10.1111/lang.12479](https://doi.org/10.1111/lang.12479).

4. **Within-session relearning can be useful but is not a substitute for later
   review.** An L2 vocabulary experiment compared initial and relearning gaps
   and explicitly examined the trade-off between spacing costs and benefits.
   This is a reason to allow a small amount of immediate supported recovery when
   the learner is stuck, while still scheduling a later, less-cued retrieval.
   [Nakata et al., 2023, *Language Learning*, DOI
   10.1111/lang.12553](https://doi.org/10.1111/lang.12553).

5. **Interleaving is a transfer/retrieval decision, not a default mixer.** A
   controlled study found that interleaving retrieval practice improved science
   learning; it is relevant evidence for making learners select among related
   possibilities instead of repeating one blocked pattern. It is not direct
   evidence that every language activity or unrelated topic should be shuffled.
   [Sana & Yan, 2022, *Psychological Science*, DOI
   10.1177/09567976211057507](https://doi.org/10.1177/09567976211057507).

6. **Language-specific interleaving evidence exists for pronunciation.** A
   published foreign-language pronunciation experiment directly compares
   interleaving with blocking, so pronunciation/listening decisions should be
   checked against that result rather than generalized from motor or science
   learning. [Carpenter & Mueller, 2013, *Memory & Cognition*, DOI
   10.3758/s13421-012-0291-4](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13421-012-0291-4).

## Implications for the Wayfinder decision

- Use **delayed, effortful retrieval** as the core evidence event. Record the
  task, cue level, context, and whether the learner could use the target, not
  only whether they recognized a definition.
- Make review **adaptive to both observed stability and the next-use horizon**:
  recent errors get a short supported retry; successful items return later in a
  less-cued and varied context. Do not hard-code an expanding SRS schedule.
- For a 10–40 minute session, reserve a small retrieval/review slice, then use
  the remainder for new input and communicative use. A short session can still
  contribute to long-term retention if later sessions supply the delay.
- Interleave only after a minimal representation is available. Mix closely
  confusable forms, contexts, or skills to force discrimination and transfer;
  keep a short blocked or constrained phase when the learner cannot yet perform
  the target. Avoid random mixing that makes an A1 task incomprehensible.
- Measure **delayed transfer** (new sentence, changed context, listening or
  speaking use) separately from immediate accuracy. If delayed performance is
  absent, the item is not mastered even if the lesson felt easy.

## Limits / unresolved research

These sources support principles, not exact intervals, item counts, or a mastery
threshold for this learner. The map still needs a decision on operational
thresholds, error categories, cue fading, and how `/teach` records review state.
Evidence for long-term spontaneous speaking and multi-speaker work calls cannot
be inferred from vocabulary-memory studies and should be researched separately.
