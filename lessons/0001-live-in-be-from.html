Where someone lives vs where someone is from

Use live in for a current place. Use be from for origin.

I live in Tbilisi. I am from Russia.

Pavel lives in Tbilisi. Pavel is from Russia.

They live in Tbilisi. They are from Russia.

Why: live describes a current location; be from describes origin. With one person, live becomes lives, but be becomes is.

Try it

Write four short sentences:

  1. where you live;
  2. where you are from;
  3. where Pavel lives;
  4. where Pavel is from.

Use your first attempt. Then ask a follow-up question if anything is unclear.

Reference: Cambridge Learner’s Dictionary: live and be.
